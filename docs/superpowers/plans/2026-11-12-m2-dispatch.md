# M2 Dispatch Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** Once an incident reaches T2, alert the right responders, in rings and along an alert ladder, until one per role accepts. Forward to a health centre when nobody does, fail safe to a coordinator at every step, and let a coordinator suspend live dispatch under the stopping rules. This proves scenarios 1 (to acceptance), 2, 7–11 and 14.

**Architecture:** Each dispatch decision is a pure planner, `Plan(state, now) → (actions, nextWake)`, in `internal/dispatch` and tested with `testing/synctest`. A thin, leader-elected scheduler in `cmd/dispatch-worker` executes the plans: due timers in PostgreSQL, `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`, idempotent actions. Alerts, attempts and assignments are rows written through SECURITY DEFINER functions. First acceptance per role is enforced by a partial unique index, and every state change is also an incident event on the audit trail. Channels are ports with adapters:
- SMS: Africa's Talking, through the egress allowlist;
- network-initiated USSD push: disabled until written confirmation (O2);
- outbound voice: ARI originate in ivr-controller;
- the USSD responder menu: intake-gw.

**Tech Stack:** Go 1.27.4, PostgreSQL 18.6 (`WITHOUT OVERLAPS` temporal keys, partial unique indexes), the existing ARI client and fake, and the Africa's Talking SMS API.

**Spec:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-12-sauti-dispatch-design.md` (§4.2–4.3, §7.1–7.5, §11 M2); Build Prompt §10.4–10.5, §10.11, §14; Engineering Guide §6, §14. Roadmap phase 3.

## Global Constraints

- **Hard constraints (Build Prompt §3).**
  - Deterministic dispatch; no model ranks responders (3).
  - Server-side time; T3 is the first alert and T4 the first acceptance, both set by the database (4).
  - The caller's number is never in a responder-facing payload, log or SMS (5).
  - No patient data on messaging apps; SMS carries incident number, colour, category, ward and landmark only (decision 1), never a name or clinical field (6).
  - Dispatch never waits on payments, analytics or monitoring, and every automated step fails safe to a human (14).
- **Study mode.** `dispatch.mode: protocol` keeps dispatch at ring 1 and escalates to the coordinator until the amendment for decisions 1, 2 and 6 is approved; `amended` enables rings to 2.5 km and forwarding. Both modes are built and tested.
- **Parameters** (`config/gated/dispatch.yaml`, gated): ring step 500 m, ring interval 60 s, maximum radius 2.5 km (ring 5), cascade timeout 90 s, red parallel count 3 per role, forwarding acknowledgement 60 s, voice fallback 20 s. The ranking order follows decision 4 (OPEN, safe default), and its version is stamped on every incident's dispatch.
- **Alerts by severity.** Red: the top 3 of each role in parallel, and at each ring step the top 3 newly eligible. Yellow and green: one responder at a time, moving on after 90 s, each next pick taken from the current (possibly wider) ring. Each role searches on its own. First acceptance wins per role; the others are stood down by SMS; a late accept hears "already taken".
- **Decline reasons** (decision 6) are closed lists per role whose last option is Other, versioned behind the amendment gate. A time-out is "no response" and has no reason.
- **Suspension (O6, user decision 2026-11-12).** Intake continues and records are kept; no responder is alerted; every incident is escalated to the coordinator. The caller's closing message is a PENDING placeholder, so production refuses to enable suspension until the Medical Director and PI approve its wording. The stopping rule computed from system data is: red dispatch failure above 15% across 30 consecutive red incidents (Guide §14). It suspends automatically. Reportable events are a dated register, and resuming needs staff action that is logged.
- **Merged calls are never dispatched.** A call merged at T2 in the same transaction as its `dispatch_ready` (M1) is never alerted for; dispatch follows the survivor.
- **Placeholders.** Responder data in development is synthetic. SMS and voice copy is a placeholder marked `PENDING`, and production refuses it. No real phone numbers appear anywhere in the repository.

## Review Focus

1. **A crash or database failover between "decide to alert" and "alert sent"** (scenario 14): no lost and no duplicate alert. Attempts are recorded before the provider call, and an attempt whose result is unknown is never re-sent automatically (the next rung covers it).
2. **Two responders accepting the same role at the same instant:** exactly one assignment; the other hears "already taken" and nothing is double-paid later.
3. **No eligible responder at all,** including an empty ring 1, everyone unavailable, or protocol mode: the coordinator is alerted immediately, and dispatch never ends silently.
4. **A responder's availability expiring or a gate lapsing mid-cascade:** never alerted after it lapses; the 8-hour expiry reminder SMS is sent once.
5. **Suspension switched on mid-cascade:** no new alert goes out after the switch; incidents in flight are escalated, never dropped.

## Part A — Registry, eligibility, ranking, planners, scheduler (branch `m2-1-core`)

### Task 1: Responder registry and availability
- `op.responders`: role `VHT` or `TRANSPORTER`; name and phone sealed (identifiers class), plus a phone MAC; ward; stage; `certified_at`, `insured_until`, `kit_ok`, `research_consent`, `status`. Writes only through SECURITY DEFINER functions.
- `op.availability`: a period `tstzrange` with `WITHOUT OVERLAPS` per responder, and a source (USSD, IVR or console). Expiry after 8 h (decision 5), with a reminder SMS before expiry.
- Stages and health facilities extend the gazetteer: `stages` (ID, ward, point, cell) and `facilities` (name, public phone as a reference identifier, point, cell, `forwarding_enabled`). All are versioned and frozen like landmarks.
- `sautictl responder add|gate|available` (dev and test only) and a synthetic fixture loader.
- Tests:
  - overlapping availability rejected by the database;
  - expiry computed on the database clock;
  - gate lapses (insurance date, kit) make a responder ineligible;
  - no phone or name in any event payload.

### Task 2: Eligibility and ranking (pure)
- `dispatch.Eligible(responders, now, ring cells)`: certified, insured, kit OK, availability open, and stage cell inside the ring.
- `dispatch.Rank(eligible, incident cell, history)` in the configured order: zone distance (Chebyshev), then response history (accept rate and last response time), then least recently dispatched; ties broken by ID. The ranking version is returned.
- Tests:
  - table tests for each criterion and tie;
  - the ranking version stamped;
  - the order is total and stable.

### Task 3: Planners (pure, synctest)
- `dispatch.State{Incident, Severity, Mode, Ring, Alerts per role, Assignments, Forwarding, Suspended}`; `Plan(state, now) (actions []Action, nextWake time.Time)`.
- Actions: `Alert{role, responder, ring, wave}`, `Escalate{reason}`, `Forward{facility}`, `StandDown{alert}`, `CallerSMS{kind}`, `Close`.
- Red: parallel top 3 per role, and the top 3 newly eligible at each ring step. Yellow and green: a cascade, one at a time, 90 s each, each next pick from the current ring.
- Rings widen every 60 s to ring 5 (amended mode only). Protocol mode stays at ring 1 and escalates when ring 1 is exhausted, or after 3 min for red.
- At ring 5 with no acceptance: forward to the nearest facility with `forwarding_enabled`. If it has not acknowledged within 60 s, try the next nearest. The coordinator is alerted at the moment of forwarding. The search continues until an acceptance or a coordinator closure.
- Suspended: no alerts, and escalate.
- Tests (synctest, fake clock) covering:
  - every row of Build Prompt §10.4;
  - scenario 2 (cascade depth 3);
  - scenario 7 (rings every 60 s to 2.5 km, forward, coordinator);
  - scenario 8 (no acknowledgement in 60 s → next facility);
  - the protocol-mode equivalents;
  - suspension mid-cascade;
  - empty eligibility → immediate escalation.

### Task 4: Dispatch store and the single write path
- Tables:
  - `op.alerts`: incident, responder, role, ring, wave, `sent_at` (first = T3), outcome (`accepted`, `declined`, `no_response`, `stood_down`), `decline_reason`, `responded_at`;
  - `op.alert_attempts`: alert, channel (`ussd_push`, `sms`, `voice`, `ussd_pull`), status (`pending` → `claimed` → `sent`, `failed` or `unknown`), provider reference;
  - `op.assignments`, with a partial unique index on (incident, role) for active assignments;
  - `op.forwards`;
  - `op.dispatch_timers` (incident, kind, due_at).
- Functions:
  - `op.issue_alert`: appends `alert.issued` (T3 is first-wins);
  - `op.accept_alert`: first wins; appends `responder.accepted` (T4); returns `already_taken` otherwise;
  - `op.decline_alert` (reason code checked against the configured list);
  - `op.record_attempt`;
  - `op.schedule` / `op.claim_due_timers`.
- Grants: `op_dispatch` gets dispatch writes; `op_intake` and `op_ivr` get only accept and decline through the functions.
- Tests:
  - concurrent accepts → one assignment (review focus 2);
  - Go/SQL parity for the new events;
  - a merged incident is never alertable.

### Task 5: dispatch-worker and the scheduler
- `cmd/dispatch-worker`:
  - a leader-elected loop that claims due timers and incidents newly in `dispatching` (non-merged);
  - it loads state, runs `Plan`, and executes the actions through ports (Alerter, Escalator, Forwarder, Notifier), all idempotent: keys `dispatch:<incident>:<role>:<ring>:<wave>:<responder>`;
  - it writes the next wake.
- Escalation port: `incident.escalated`, plus a coordinator task. Voice delivery arrives in Part B.
- Tests:
  - crash and restart between plan and execution → no duplicate alert (review focus 1);
  - a database failover mid-cascade, by connection kill and reconnect → scenario 14 holds;
  - suspension switched on mid-cascade (review focus 5).

### Task 6: Suspension control and stopping rules
- `op.dispatch_control`: a single row; suspended or active, reason, by-staff, at.
- `op.suspend_dispatch` / `op.resume_dispatch`: console role, staff identity, events on a system log incident or a control audit entry.
- Rolling window over the last 30 red incidents: failure means no acceptance before closure or escalation; above 15% → automatic suspension. A reportable-events register.
- Intake reads the suspension state: the caller's closing message becomes the PENDING placeholder, and the incident is escalated.
- `sautictl dispatch suspend|resume|status`.
- Tests:
  - window arithmetic at the boundaries (4 of 30 = 13.3% passes; 5 of 30 fails);
  - the placeholder blocks production;
  - suspension stops alerts and escalates.

### Task 7: Part A docs and gates
- ADR-0024: dispatch as pure planners plus a PostgreSQL scheduler. This replaces DBOS from spec §4.2 and §14; see the rulings.
- ADR-0025: alerts, attempts and first-accept-wins.
- ADR-0026: suspension and stopping rules (O6).
- Runbooks: dispatch-worker-down, suspension-and-resumption, no-eligible-responders.
- Updated docs: events, configuration, stores, traceability, model card (ranking), threats.

## Part B — Channels (branch `m2-2-channels`)

### Task 8: SMS
- An `sms` port and an Africa's Talking SMS adapter, through egress, with a bounded timeout and delivery reports.
- Templates in gated config, with a compliance test: no patient name, no clinical field, and no caller number in any responder SMS (Build Prompt §16).
- The SMS set: alert SMS, stand-down, caller "helper on the way" (no responder name or number), caller forwarding SMS (centre name and public number), availability-expiry reminder. All copy is PENDING.

### Task 9: Responder USSD menu (pull) and decline reasons
- intake-gw recognises a registered responder by phone MAC on the responder service code.
- The menu: Active alert (1 Accept, 2 Decline) → decline reason (the closed list per role, 6 Other); availability on and off.
- A late accept hears "already taken".
- Tests: scenario 10 (decline reason 3 stored and linked); concurrent accepts across channels.

### Task 10: Outbound voice alerts and the ladder
- ivr-controller originates calls through ARI (a new client call: `originate`, with no recording).
- Prompts: press 1 accept, 2 decline (then the reason), 3 repeat.
- The ladder: USSD push (port, disabled) and SMS at 0 s; voice at 20 s without an answer; the USSD pull menu at any time.
- Tests: scenario 9 (push unanswered → voice at 20 s → accepted by voice); every attempt logged per channel.

### Task 11: Forwarding and coordinator calls
- Health-centre forwarding call: press 1 to acknowledge; within 60 s, or move to the next facility. An SMS to the centre carries incident number, colour, category, ward and landmark. The caller gets an SMS with the centre's name and public number.
- Coordinator escalation by voice: durable and retried, with a configured coordinator dial target (placeholder).
- Tests: scenario 7 and scenario 8 end to end with FakeARI and a fake SMS gateway; scenario 11 (rule error → coordinator voice call).

### Task 12: Part B docs and gates
- ADR-0027: channels and the alert ladder (O2: USSD push disabled until written confirmation).
- Runbooks: SMS gateway down, voice alerts failing.
- Reference updates; the SMS content compliance test in traceability (hard constraint 6).

## Part C — Masked bridge, surge, red end-to-end (branch `m2-3-bridge`)

### Task 13: Masked bridge (Build Prompt §10.5)
- The responder calls the toll-free bridge number. ivr-controller recognises the responder (phone MAC) and the active assignment, asks for the last 4 digits if there are several, and bridges to the caller. The caller sees only the Sauti number.
- The caller can call back the same way.
- `op.bridge_calls`: time, duration, result. Nothing is recorded.
- The ARI client gains bridge-mixing calls and still has no recording.
- Tests: the caller's number never reaches the responder leg, logs or payloads; several assignments → last-4 prompt.

### Task 14: Surge, red end to end, after-acceptance messages
- Surge: when incidents outnumber responders, incidents queue by severity, then T2; nothing is dropped. A surge flag feeds the console (M5).
- After acceptance: the alert channel repeats the landmark and the bridge instructions; the caller gets the "helper on the way" SMS. If the responder goes silent, the caller is asked at set intervals whether help has arrived; no confirmation escalates (the interval is config).
- E2E: scenario 1 to acceptance (red trauma: parallel alerts, first accept per role, the rest stood down); scenario 2 (yellow obstetric cascade to depth 3; the transporter accepts at once); scenario 14 (database failover mid-cascade).

### Task 15: Part C docs and gates
- ADR-0028: masked bridge.
- Runbooks: bridge failing, surge.
- Traceability: scenarios 1, 2, 7–11, 14.
- Threats: forged acceptance, a responder impersonating another, bridge exposure of the caller's number.

## Rulings carried into execution
- **Pure planners plus a PostgreSQL timer table and a leader-elected scheduler, instead of DBOS Transact** (spec §4.2, §14).
  - Why: every dispatch fact already lives in the operational store under its single write path, with idempotency keys and audit events. DBOS would add a second, serialised workflow state outside that path and outside the verifiers. Pure planners also make every timer deterministic under `testing/synctest` (Build Prompt §16).
  - Cost if wrong: the planners are executor-agnostic; DBOS could drive them later.
- **An alert attempt with an unknown result is never re-sent automatically; the next rung covers it.** Why: a duplicate SMS or call to a responder is avoided (scenario 14). Cost if wrong: one rung later.
- **USSD push stays behind a disabled port (O2).** Why: no documented Africa's Talking network-initiated USSD API. The ladder runs SMS at 0 s and voice at 20 s. Cost if wrong: adding an adapter.
